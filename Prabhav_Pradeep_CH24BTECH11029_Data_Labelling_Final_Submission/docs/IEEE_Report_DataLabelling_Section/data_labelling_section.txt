I. DATA LABELLING

Strategy.
Training labels are produced automatically by a classical labeller on videos 1
and 2 (105 frames). The final (V4) dataset has 1,487 tiles of 256 x 256 px and
13,507 granules. Each tile has a granule mask, granule and cell instance images,
and YOLO boxes. The tiles are split into 5 folds grouped by grid position, so the
same cells never appear in both training and test data. Video 3 is never used for
training. From it, 19 tiles (5 frames, 7 positions) were uploaded to Roboflow,
and 8 of them (102 granules) were outlined manually with polygons (Fig. 1). These
hand labels are the fixed answer key for every label version and every network.
A prediction is a true positive (TP) if its IoU with a hand granule is at least
0.5; capture is the share of hand granules of which at least half the pixels are
predicted.

[Fig. 1 - figures/hand_vs_auto.png]
Held-out video-3 tiles: manual and automatic (V4) labels (red = granule).

Cell mask.
Each pixel is described by x = ( sqrt(I), log(1 + D) ), where I in [0, 1] is the
intensity and D the optical-flow magnitude. A three-component Gaussian mixture is
fitted to 2 x 10^5 sampled pixels. The two darkest components are background; the
third is cell:

    P(cell | x) = [ pi_c N(x; mu_c, Sigma_c) / sum_{j=1..3} pi_j N(x; mu_j, Sigma_j) ]
                  * 1 / (1 + exp(-(I - I0) / 0.005))                          (1)

The sigmoid gate stops motion alone from making a dark pixel a cell pixel. I0 is
the lowest intensity at which a still pixel (D = 0) has P > 0.5.

Top-hat contrast.
Cell brightness varies about 10x, so a global threshold fails. With I_s = G_1 * I
and pixels outside cells filled by normalised convolution, the background is the
smoothed grey-scale opening with a disk B_r:

    b = G_{r/2} * (I_s o B_r),      S = (I_s - b) / (b + 0.05)                (2)

The opening (I erosion B_r) dilation B_r removes every bright structure that B_r
does not fit inside. The top-hat I_s - b therefore keeps only the puncta, and
dividing by b makes it a relative contrast, comparable across bright and dim
cells. A granule wider than B_r is absorbed into b, so r must exceed the largest
granule radius.

Hessian blobs.
From I(p + h) ~ I + grad(I)^T h + (1/2) h^T H h, both eigenvalues
lambda_1 >= lambda_2 of H are strongly negative at a bright blob, while
lambda_1 ~ 0 on a ridge such as a cell rim. The blob response is

    B = max over sigma of  sigma^2 * max(0, -lambda_1(sigma))                (3)

where sigma^2 normalises the second derivatives across scales, so the response
peaks at blob radius ~ 1.4 sigma.

Seeds and growth.
Seeds are local maxima inside cells where

    B / (b + 0.05) > median + k * 1.4826 * MAD,     S >= S_min               (4)

The factor 1.4826 makes the MAD a consistent estimate of sigma_noise, and its 50%
breakdown point stops the granules from raising their own threshold. Each seed p
is grown by a watershed down to half its peak height, b_p + 0.5 (I_p - b_p),
within min(R, 2.5 sigma_p + 2) px of the seed. A region is kept if its area is
within the version's limits, eccentricity <= 0.95, solidity >= 0.7, and it is not
on a saturated blob. Label caches are named after their settings, so new settings
cannot silently reload old labels. Re-running the code with V1 settings gives 362
granules on a test frame, against 366 stored.

Label versions.
The labeller went through four versions (Table I, Fig. 2). After each one all
networks were retrained and scored on the same hand tiles. V1 counted every
4-400 px spot, so U-Net found most granules (recall 0.73) but 3 of every 4
detections were wrong. V2 dropped blobs under 60 px, as feedback asked for fewer
false positives. Precision doubled, but real 30-60 px granules were lost (recall
0.38). V3 restored the 30 px minimum with a stricter brightness test (recall
0.66). V4 widened the top-hat (r = 20 px) and the blob scales (sigma <= 7).
Before, granules wider than about 14 px were hollowed out or missed; now they are
outlined in full. Dice rose from 0.55 to 0.70, and V4 recovers V1's recall at
twice its precision.

Every network gains from V2 to V4 (F1 on the hand tiles): NoSkip 0.36 -> 0.48,
U-Net 0.37 -> 0.52, AttU-Net 0.35 -> 0.54, DWU-Net 0.35 -> 0.51,
DWAttU-Net 0.36 -> 0.51. The gain comes from the labels, not from one
architecture. Scored directly, the labeller itself improves from F1 0.38 (V1;
130 FP) to 0.53 (V4; P 0.58, R 0.64, 40 FP, 7 tiles), while a DoG detector
reaches 0.22 (recall 0.79 but 714 FP). On the same tiles the networks reach
0.48-0.52, about as good as the labeller they learned from. Against each
version's own labels, 5-fold U-Net F1 is 0.71, 0.62, 0.69 and 0.66 for V1-V4.
This measures how well a network copies its labeller, not label quality: V1
scores highest yet is worst on the hand tiles.

Errors by size.
Large granules are found reliably. For hand granules of at least 150 px (31
granules), U-Net recall is 0.80 and capture 99%; for 30-150 px, recall is 0.68
and capture 77%. The V1 labeller found none of the 4 granules over 400 px. False
positives are mostly tiny: 44% of U-Net's (33 of 75) are under 30 px, a size the
labels never contain. On the 5-fold sets, granules of at least 150 px have recall
0.64, and about a fifth of them are merged with a neighbour. A blob filter
deletes predicted blobs under N px, with no retraining. N was chosen on
validation tiles only, where every network peaks at N = 20. On the hand tiles,
precision rises from 0.49 to 0.56 at unchanged recall 0.71, and false positives
fall from 75 to 56. On the 5-fold test sets, precision rises from 0.64 to 0.72
and F1 from 0.66 to 0.70. At N = 40, hand-tile recall falls to 0.57.

Perimeter and time.
Hand-labelled granules have a median perimeter of 35 px, and 64% lie between 20
and 45 px. U-Net outlines are within 7% of the labeller's but 14% shorter than
hand-drawn ones. In video 1 (0-60 min), labelled granules per tile rise from 6.2
to 13.3 while their mean perimeter falls from 62 to 34 px (p < 0.001, Pearson
test on a linear fit), so many small new granules form. In video 2 (60-120 min)
both change only slightly. U-Net follows the same trends but over-counts.
